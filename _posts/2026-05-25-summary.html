hackernews · zdw · May 24, 04:14 · Discussion

EN

AMD announced that Vivado 2026.1 will no longer include Linux support in its free Basic tier, forcing developers to either purchase the Standard Edition for $1,200 per year or use Windows. The change was made quietly and has sparked debate among engineers about vendor lock‐in and ecosystem access. Community members are urging AMD to reconsider the restriction to maintain broad adoption.

This licensing shift restricts a large segment of developers, especially students and hobbyists, who rely on free Linux tools, thereby increasing vendor lock‐in risk for AMD. It may drive talent toward competing FPGA vendors such as Lattice, affecting AMD’s long‐term market share and ecosystem vitality. The move could also disrupt CI pipelines and educational workflows that depend on cross‐platform development.

The free Basic tier will be limited to Windows, and the paid Standard Edition costs at least $1,200 annually, which many users consider prohibitive. Existing node‐locked licenses may restrict usage to a single machine, and AMD has not clarified whether multi‐node licenses will be available. Community members note that the restriction could hinder cross‐compiling and CI automation that traditionally run on Linux.

Vivado is AMD’s flagship FPGA design suite used by engineers to develop and verify hardware designs on Xilinx devices. Prior to Vivado 2026.1, the free Basic tier supported Linux on x86‐64 systems, allowing students, hobbyists, and small teams to develop without cost. The recent removal of Linux support means developers must either upgrade to a paid Standard Edition or switch to a Windows environment, raising concerns about vendor lock‐in. Vendor lock‐in refers to a situation where customers become dependent on a single vendor’s ecosystem, limiting their ability to switch to alternatives without high switching costs, as defined by multiple sources. This change could affect the broader FPGA market by narrowing the accessible developer base and encouraging migration to competing tools.

Hackers exploit chatbot personalities to bypass security.

rss · The Verge · May 24, 12:00

EN

Hackers are learning to exploit the predefined personalities of AI chatbots, using tailored prompts that manipulate the bot’s tone and role to bypass safety filters. These attacks, which include prompt injection techniques, allow adversaries to extract data or force the chatbot to perform unintended actions, turning a conversational style into a security vulnerability.

This development highlights a new attack surface in AI security, as the personality traits that make chatbots engaging also provide leverage for malicious actors to bypass safeguards. Consequently, users' privacy and trust may be compromised, and companies will need to redesign chatbot architectures to protect against personality‐based prompt injection.

The attacks leverage the chatbot’s personality traits—such as its tone, role, and conversational style—to craft prompts that bypass built‐in safety filters, a technique known as prompt injection. Because the personality is encoded in the model’s instructions, longer or overly detailed personality descriptions can make the model more susceptible, and the exploit works even when the chatbot has web‐browsing capabilities that allow indirect prompt injection from external content.

Chatbot personality refers to the human‐like traits and tone that developers assign to an AI assistant to make interactions feel consistent and engaging, as described in several 2026 guides. These personalities are created through carefully crafted prompts that define the bot’s voice, role, and behavioral style, which can be exploited by attackers using prompt injection techniques. Prompt injection, or AI jailbreaking, involves crafting inputs that trick the model into ignoring its safety constraints, a risk that becomes more serious when the model can browse the web and ingest external content. The Verge’s article, part of the weekly Stepback newsletter, reports on this emerging security concern in the rapidly evolving AI landscape.

DeepSeek makes a permanent 75% discount on its V4‐Pro AI model

rss · Bloomberg Technology · May 23, 10:28

EN

DeepSeek announced that it will permanently offer a 75% discount on its flagship V4‐Pro AI model, reducing the price for developers to one‐quarter of the original level. The company said the discount will continue indefinitely, making the model more affordable for developers worldwide. This decision follows the recent release of the V4‐Pro model in April 2026.

The permanent 75% discount dramatically lowers the cost barrier for developers, accelerating AI application deployment and intensifying competition in the AI market. It may pressure established AI hardware and model providers, such as Nvidia, to adjust pricing or improve value propositions. Developers and startups will gain greater access to a high‐performing open‐weight model, potentially reshaping the AI ecosystem.

The V4‐Pro model comprises 1.6 trillion parameters with 49 billion activated, uses hybrid attention to cut inference FLOPs by 27% and KV cache by 10% at a one‐million‐token context. DeepSeek’s pricing will stay at one‐quarter of the original level indefinitely, a permanent reduction from the previous steep discount. The model is open‐weight, meaning its parameters are publicly shared, though the training data remains proprietary.

DeepSeek is a Chinese AI startup founded in July 2023 by Liang Wenfeng, the co‐founder of the hedge fund High‐Flyer, and it specializes in large language models. Since its January 2025 launch of the open‐source R1 model, DeepSeek has released several increasingly capable models, including the V4‐Pro flagship announced in April 2026. The company claims to have trained its models at a fraction of the cost of rivals, using techniques such as mixture‐of‐experts and fewer high‐end chips, which has sparked industry shock and a reported $600 billion drop in Nvidia’s market value.
