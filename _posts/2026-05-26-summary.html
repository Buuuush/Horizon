Tech Researchers Sue Trump Administration Over Online Safety Future

rss · MIT Technology Review · May 21, 12:10

EN

Tech researchers have filed a lawsuit against the Trump administration to challenge its approach to online safety and the future of Section 230. The administration has been targeting researchers who study hate speech for months, raising concerns about censorship and the legal protection afforded to online platforms.

The lawsuit could reshape the legal framework governing online content and may limit the scope of Section 230 immunity, affecting how platforms moderate hate speech. If successful, it could deter research on hate speech and alter the balance between free expression and platform responsibility, impacting both tech companies and civil society.

The case centers on the Trump administration's recent moves to curtail online safety protections, including attempts to modify or revoke Section 230, which grants immunity to interactive computer services for user‐generated content. Section 230 was originally enacted in 1996 and was amended in 2018 by the FOSTA‐SESTA Act to address sex‐trafficking content, and it remains a cornerstone of U.S. internet law.

Section 230 is a provision of the Communications Decency Act of 1996 that grants legal immunity to online platforms for content posted by users, a principle established after early lawsuits in the 1990s. The law was upheld by the Supreme Court in Reno v. ACLU (1997) and has been reinforced by subsequent court decisions, making it central to the operation of social media and other interactive services. In recent years, the Trump administration has pursued aggressive actions against researchers studying hate speech, arguing that the current legal framework enables harmful content, while critics warn this threatens research and free expression. The pending lawsuit by tech researchers seeks to protect the integrity of online safety research and the continued application of Section 230.
