MIT Technology Review hosted a roundtable with editor in chief Mat Honan, senior AI editor Will Douglas Heaven, and an AI reporter to discuss whether AI systems can learn to understand the world beyond current large language models. This discussion highlights a pivotal shift in AI research toward world models, which could enable systems to plan, reason, and act more reliably, thereby improving safety and alignment. If successful, such models may reduce reliance on massive data and mitigate risks associated with misaligned behavior in advanced AI systems.

The roundtable featured experts including Mat Honan, Will Douglas Heaven, and an AI reporter, focusing on world models that build internal representations of environments and predict how they change over time. These models, rooted in research dating back to the 1990s, are now being applied to robotics, autonomous driving, and interactive video generation, and major AI firms are investing heavily in their development.

World models are machine learning systems that construct an internal representation of an environment and predict how that environment evolves in response to actions, differing from classifiers or generators that only produce outputs. The concept dates back to the 1990s and has been used in robotics, autonomous vehicles, and video generation, while recent advances have brought them to the forefront of AI research as a potential successor to large language models. AI alignment concerns, which aim to ensure systems pursue human‐intended goals, are closely linked to world models because a system that understands the world more accurately is less likely to develop unintended behaviors. Ongoing debates among leading researchers and company leaders reflect both optimism about new capabilities and caution about safety implications.
